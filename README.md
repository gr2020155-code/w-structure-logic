## Abstract

In W-structure, self-consistency does not arise through choice, a search through alternatives, or the elimination of contradictions. A non-self-consistent actualization is not another state of the Universe: it is not realized as a state. Logic therefore does not govern existence from outside; logical laws express the necessary relations of what exists.

The article distinguishes three subjects: ontological actualization, its mathematical representation, and the knowledge available to a subsystem. For a finite event record, a language of statements about realization is constructed. The meanings of "and", "or", persistent exclusion and persistent implication are obtained from explicitly stated semantic requirements on regions of realization, and the laws are then proved directly from these meanings: non-contradiction, the laws of persistent implication, the weak law of excluded middle on the cuts of one history, Dummett's law for the language of a single local system, and classical determinacy only at the complete cut. For the class of all finite event records, the logic is exactly KC.

The scope of Gödel's incompleteness theorems is stated separately: the Universe is not automatically a formal calculus, but an algorithmic description of it is not exempt from mathematical theorems merely because it describes W. Every representational step that is not proved is listed explicitly (section 12.3).
